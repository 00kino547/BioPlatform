## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Resale mode `permitted` — publish a resale *permission*, not just prohibitions** — the paid-invite store could already publish a resale prohibition (`legal`) or prohibit and track (`enforced`), and `off` meant "the instance states nothing". There was no way to say the opposite of `legal`: an instance that *allows* members to sell their invite codes had to publish silence, because the storefront and the legal pages derived their wording from `mode !== "off"`. A fourth value, **`permitted`**, publishes an explicit clause that members **may** resell, re-list or transfer codes they hold — bought or earned — with the limits spelled out: the buyer of a code becomes responsible for registering with it, the operator may still suspend for abuse (including revoking the codes of the offending purchase), and resale transfers neither membership, subscription nor billing history, nor does it remove the buyer's refund rights. The clause is deliberately written to describe what the platform actually does, so it cannot contradict the implementation: invite codes are plain bearer secrets, so nothing here prevents a transfer, and `enforced` still only records origin rather than blocking sharing. `off` and `permitted` both leave `sellingAllowed: true` and differ solely in publication; `legal`/`enforced` keep `sellingAllowed: false`. The published stance is now derived once in `lib/inviteService.ts` (`InviteResalePolicy.clause` → `none | permitted | prohibited`) and sent on both `/api/policy/context` (`resalePermitted`, `resaleRestricted`) and the public `/api/invite-purchases/config` (`resale.clause`), so the Terms, the Privacy Policy and the storefront cannot disagree — and the storefront can no longer print "this instance does not permit invite codes to be resold" for an instance that permits it. Admin **Invite Codes → Paid invite credits** gained the option with an explanation of each value. Terms §Purchased Invites and Privacy §Purchased Invites gained the matching clauses.
- **Legal snapshots are invalidated on the writes that change them** — `/api/policy/context` is cached for 60 s, so changing the resale mode, the generation switch or the store switch left the published legal page describing the previous stance for up to a minute after the admin saw "Saved". `lib/policyContextCache.ts` owns the key and `clearPolicyContextCache()` is now called from each of those admin writes; a stale legal page is worse than a marginally slower save.
- **Pay-by-hand instructions (admin-editable, and shared with the invite store)** — the paid-invite storefront offered a **MANUAL** method that told a buyer only "the operator confirms this by hand", with nowhere for the operator to say *where to send the money*: the invite admin card had no editor at all, so the one setting that could have carried those instructions (`orders.contactMethod` / `orders.contactValue`) was reachable only from the **Orders** tab. That setting now moves to `lib/contactConfig.ts` (shared by both routes instead of being duplicated inside `routes/orders.ts`), is edited by one reusable `ManualPaymentInstructionsEditor` rendered in **both** the invite card and the Orders tab, and is published on `GET /api/invite-purchases/config` as `manualPayment` (`method`, `value`, `configured`). `configured` is derived server-side from the stored setting rather than from the presence of the choice, so a method selected with an empty value cannot leave a buyer stuck: **the storefront hides "pay by hand" entirely while no usable instructions exist** instead of offering a checkout that cannot be paid, and the editor shows an amber warning so a half-finished setting cannot silently remove a payment method. When instructions are published they are shown inline at checkout ("send payment to …, then reference your order"), stating the real consequence that the operator confirms by hand, so it is not instant. The value is sanitized and capped at 300 characters, as before.
- **"Buy an invite" reachable from where people get stranded** — registration is invite-only, so Register and Login dead-ended anyone without a code, and the landing FAQ claimed the only ways in were "a handpicked invitation from our staff" or "being requested by an existing member" — untrue once the store was open, with no route offered to the person who has nobody to ask. A shared `BuyInviteModal` opens the same storefront in place with the address the visitor already typed pre-filled (register seeds it from the email field; login seeds it only when its username-or-email field actually contains an `@`, so a username is never used as a delivery address), with Escape/backdrop dismissal, focus moved into the dialog and returned to the trigger on close, and background scroll locked. It is reachable from **Register** (under the invite-code field), **Login**, the landing **hero** and the FAQ answer that mentions buying, and renders nothing at all when the store is closed so no page advertises an offer that is not being made. Every entry point is also a plain `href` to `/invites` (the link carries `?email=` so following it does not mean retyping the address), keeping the crawlable, shareable, no-JavaScript path alongside the popup; `/invites` accepts and validates that parameter. The FAQ invite answer is now built from the live policy context, so it names buying when the store is open and keeps the member-request wording when it is not. `lib/emailHint.ts` holds the shared "is this usable as a delivery address" rule.

- **Operator auto-accept of the Terms / Privacy Policy (`POLICY_ADMIN_AUTO_ACCEPT`)** — the consent gate refuses every authenticated call until an account accepts the current `POLICY_VERSIONS`, and that included the **admin** account: bumping the policy versions locked the operator out of the panel they administer (found live while verifying the invite store, where the first admin call returned `policies_outdated`). Accounts whose role slug is listed in `POLICY_ADMIN_AUTO_ACCEPT_ROLES` (default: the single built-in `admin`) are now recorded as accepting a new version automatically — but only after the **same 30-day review window** (`DEEMED_ACCEPTANCE_DAYS`) every other account gets, never on the day it is published, and only once the account is used again afterwards. The acceptance is **written to the user's row** with a new `policies_auto_accepted` marker recording that the platform recorded it rather than a person clicking accept; `POST /api/auth/accept-policies` clears that marker, so a deliberate acceptance is never reported as automatic. `GET /api/auth/me` and the gate now read consent through the **same** `readConsentState` helper (they computed it independently, so the SPA could be told "you are current" while the API was still refusing the account) and both surface `autoAccepted`. `GET /api/admin/policy/status` returns `operatorAutoAccept` (`{ enabled, roles, windowOpen, cutoff }`) and **Admin → Legal** states the rule in force, naming the env vars. Set `POLICY_ADMIN_AUTO_ACCEPT=false` to require staff to click through like anyone else. Published in Terms §14 and Privacy §8. New `tests/policy-auto-accept.test.ts` (7 cases), a test-only `resetEnvForTests()`, and an injectable clock on `readConsentState`. Migration `20261002120000_policy_admin_auto_accept`.
- **Guest invite deliveries can be handed over manually when mail is unavailable** — a guest purchase has no account to return to, so the codes must reach the buyer, and an instance with SMTP off could confirm payment and then have no way at all to deliver. `POST /api/admin/invite-purchases/:id/claim-link` (`invites.manage`) mints a fresh signed claim URL for one order and returns `{ url, expiresInDays }` — never the raw codes — for the operator to send by chat, DM or a different mailbox. `GET /api/admin/invite-purchase-settings` reports `emailConfigured`, and the admin invite card shows a prominent warning while purchases are open with mail unavailable, names the fix (configure a real mailbox via `SMTP_*`) and offers **Copy claim link**; the guest status page advises checking spam after a purchase. The order is **not** marked as emailed, so the automatic path still retries once mail is configured, and the codes stay hidden until `PAID`.
- **Compose deploy-path parity guard** — `docker-compose.yml` and `docker-compose.prebuilt.yml` are meant to be interchangeable deploy paths (source build vs published image) and had drifted by **63 backend variables**, so an operator following the documented prebuilt path silently ran with schema defaults for billing prices, invite packs and more, with nothing failing loudly. Both files now forward an identical backend environment (151 keys) and `scripts/check-compose-env-parity.mjs` (`pnpm compose:check`) compares the **resolved** configs and fails on any difference; wired into `.github/workflows/tests.yml`. The missing variables — including the pre-existing `POLICY_NOTICE_AUTO_EMAIL`, which no compose file forwarded at all — were added to both.
- **Paid invite credits (instance store, guest + member checkout)** — invites can now be sold. The operator prices fixed bundles with `INVITE_PRICE_PACKS` (`quantity:priceCents` pairs, whole units 1–50, whole cents) and flips one switch in **Admin → Invite Codes → Paid invite credits** (`GET/PUT /api/admin/invite-purchase-settings`, `invites.manage`). The store only reads as open when the switch is on **and** at least one pack parses, so an enabled toggle with no prices never advertises a purchase. **No bonuses and no rounding**: a pack of 10 is exactly 10 credits and only the configured quantities can be bought. A separate three-valued **resale mode** (`off` / `legal` / `enforced`) is published in the Terms and Privacy pages and is independent of the switch, so an instance can sell invites while its terms forbid reselling them; `GET /api/invite-purchases/resellable` lists the caller's own earned (non-purchased) codes for that posture.
  **Guest checkout** — registration is invite-only, so the whole purchase works with no account. `POST /api/invite-purchases` works with or without a bearer token (10/h per IP) and supports `STRIPE`, `PAYPAL`, the configured crypto gateway and operator-confirmed `MANUAL`. A member purchase is credited to the account as a **permanent balance**; a guest purchase is minted as expiring codes (`INVITE_PURCHASE_CODE_TTL_DAYS`, default 30) and emailed once with a private claim link (`/invites/purchased/:orderId?token=`). Redeeming one of those codes during registration **adopts the rest of the same order** for the new account — wired into local registration and all three OAuth registration paths — so a paid purchase is never orphaned one code at a time.
  **Provenance & refunds** — new `InviteCreditGrant` / `InviteCreditSource` ledger ties every credit and code to the order that paid for it (`InviteCode.purchased`, `purchasedAt`, `sourceGrantId`, nullable `guestOwnedById`). Credits are drawn from a specific grant, and a refund revokes exactly what its own order still holds — unused credits deleted, unused codes revoked — while anything already redeemed is left alone, because revoking that would mean deleting a real account. Bans reconcile against the ledger too: earned credits are zeroed, **purchased credits survive a ban** (a ban must not confiscate money the user already spent), purchased guest codes stay usable for a 14-day window, and a banned account can buy nothing. Migration `apps/backend/prisma/migrations/20261001120000_invite_credit_ledger/migration.sql` (applied to the test + preview DBs; `InviteCreditGrant.orderId` is TEXT so prefixed `invites-…` ids fit). OpenAPI (7 paths) + en/es api + admin-guide + env docs updated. Frontend: public `/invites` store, `/invites/purchased/:orderId` claim page, a store card in the user **Invites** tab (hidden while banned, since the backend would refuse), and **PURCHASED** code badges. `tests/invite-purchases.test.ts` (37 cases); **517/517** backend tests pass.
- **Instance-owner legal policy status + change notice (Admin → Legal)** — bumping `POLICY_VERSIONS` now blocks every out-of-date account (see Security below), so the owner needs to see how many people are affected and tell them. `lib/policyNotice.ts` exposes `GET /api/admin/policy/status` (`settings.manage`): live Terms/Privacy versions, effective date, the 30-day deemed-acceptance cutoff, how many accounts have yet to accept out of the total, whether the change is still unannounced, the last notified version pair, an optional owner note, whether the automatic notice is armed, and whether mail is usable at all. `PUT /api/admin/policy/note` stores that note (≤ 500 chars) in `SystemSetting`; it is included in the email so users know *why* they are being asked again. `POST /api/admin/policy/notify` emails every account that still has to accept — verified addresses only, 500 per run so one click cannot queue thousands, sequential, and a partial run is retried rather than abandoned. Frontend `pages/admin/AdminPolicyTab.tsx` renders version cards, the pending/total count, the cutoff, a "last notified" readout, an amber banner while a change is unannounced, the note editor and an **Email everyone** button that disables itself with an explanation when SMTP is off. State is stored in the existing `SystemSetting` table (no migration). The button works regardless of the env toggle below; the notice UI disappears when mail is unavailable.
- **`POLICY_NOTICE_AUTO_EMAIL` (automatic legal-change notice)** — new env var (default `true`) that emails every account still awaiting acceptance once at boot when the policy versions change. Deduplicated by the notified version pair stored in `SystemSetting`, so a restart loop cannot spam anybody, skipped entirely when SMTP is off, and a no-op on a fresh install with no accounts (nothing has "changed" yet). Runs unawaited and fully wrapped so a slow or failing mail relay can never delay startup or kill the process (`index.ts`). The Admin → Legal button covers the same ground on demand.

- **Instance-accurate Terms of Service and Privacy Policy** — `/terms` and `/privacy` now describe what the deployment actually does instead of hedging with "the instance operator may optionally enable …". A new secret-free public endpoint `GET /api/policy/context` returns a capability snapshot (analytics provider, sign-in providers, captcha, storage backend, payment gateways, mail transport, ACME, Discord, update check, policy version), and both pages render present/absent wording per capability: the policy names the captcha actually in use, drops the Matomo paragraph entirely when no external analytics runs, says plainly when no payment processor or crypto tipping is configured, and only mentions an own-SMTP relay where one is possible. The snapshot is booleans and provider labels only — no URLs, keys, buckets, hostnames or credentials — and a backend test asserts no configured secret can appear in the payload. Both pages now read their effective date from the same `POLICY_VERSIONS` constant the consent records use, so the displayed date and the enforced deemed-acceptance cutoff can no longer drift, and the two pages share one rendering shell that renumbers subsections from the sections actually present (this also fixed the old Terms ordering bug, where "9b. Product Shop" rendered before "9a. Tips"). If the snapshot is unreachable the pages keep their neutral wording rather than asserting that a processor is active.
- **Optional PocketBase sign-in provider (opt-in)** — an instance operator can offer PocketBase as a **sign-in provider**, and that is its only role. Nothing in the platform depends on it: accounts, links, shop, billing, newsletter, storage and admin all live in PostgreSQL, and with `POCKETBASE_URL` empty the platform boots and behaves exactly as if PocketBase were not installed. **Env** — `POCKETBASE_URL` (empty = disabled), `POCKETBASE_CLIENT_URL` (default `/api/pb-speed`), `POCKETBASE_ADMIN_EMAIL`/`POCKETBASE_ADMIN_PASSWORD` (operator superuser used for internal reads, never exposed), `POCKETBASE_AUTH_COLLECTION` (default `users`) and `POCKETBASE_OAUTH_ENABLED` (default `false`, which is also what `GET /api/features` reports to the SPA as `pocketbaseOauth`). **Flow** — the browser never posts the password to this backend: the SPA sends the identity and password to PocketBase through the same-origin proxy `POCKETBASE_CLIENT_URL` and forwards only the resulting token. **Backend** — `lib/pocketbase/config.ts` (`buildPocketbaseConfig`: trims trailing slashes, validates the URL with Zod) and `lib/pocketbase/client.ts` (small REST wrapper with a cached superuser token, authenticating against the modern `POST /api/collections/_superusers/auth-with-password` with the standard `Bearer` scheme and falling back to the legacy v0.22 `POST /api/admins/auth-with-password` with `Admin`); three endpoints under `/api/auth/oauth/pocketbase` — `GET /config` (public: `{ enabled, clientUrl, authCollection, signupRequiresInvite, twoFactorBypassAllowed }`, no secrets), `POST /exchange` (`{ token, invite? }` → the standard `OAuthExchangeResult` shapes `logged_in`, `needs_two_factor`, `needs_setup`, or `400` for a rejected or invalid PocketBase session) and `POST /link` (auth required: `{ linked, alreadyLinked }`, `400` when the PB email is unverified, `409` when the identity is already attached to another account); unlink uses the generic `DELETE /api/auth/oauth/accounts/pocketbase/:providerAccountId`. The module-level helper `provisionOAuthIdentity` (invite abuse checks + atomic consumption, referral/discount, alias-clash guard) is shared with the social signup path. PocketBase is a first-party trust domain — operator-controlled, and the caller has already proven mailbox control there — so a **PB-verified** email auto-links to the matching existing account (and verifies a matching *unverified* one) instead of returning social OAuth's `409`. `OAuthProvider`/`LinkableProvider` now include `"pocketbase"`. Startup logs the configured PocketBase status and never contacts it, so there is no boot-time dependency. **Frontend** — `PocketBaseLoginButton` on Login/Register, rendered only when the PB config reports `enabled`: an inline email/password form posted to PocketBase through `/api/pb-speed`, a second "enter your one-time code" step when PocketBase MFA is required (`auth-with-password` → 401 `mfaId` → `request-otp` → `auth-with-otp`), then the usual exchange routing (`logged_in` → dashboard, `needs_two_factor` → the existing `/login?tf=1` screen, `needs_setup` → the `/oauth/complete` completion form); provider labels wired into `OAuthComplete`, `SecurityTab` and the SSO buttons. **Infra** — nginx gained `location /api/pb-speed/` → `http://pocketbase:8090/` (the trailing slash strips the prefix, so no PocketBase port is published and no CORS is needed); `pocketbase/Dockerfile` compiles `examples/base` in Go at a pinned tag and `pocketbase/entrypoint.sh` upserts the superuser from `PB_ADMIN_EMAIL`/`PB_ADMIN_PASSWORD` before serving on 8090 behind a `/api/health` check with a `pb_data` volume, while `docker-compose.prebuilt.yml` pulls `ghcr.io/muchobien/pocketbase` (`POCKETBASE_IMAGE` override); both compose files start it as `service_started` so it never blocks the core stack. Two things that only surface when it actually runs: the compose build context is the **repo root**, so the Dockerfile copies `pocketbase/entrypoint.sh` rather than `entrypoint.sh`, and PocketBase v0.40.4 requires **go ≥ 1.27** (hence `golang:1.27-alpine`). **Deliberately not part of this release: PocketBase-backed analytics, storage and content.** An earlier branch also carried a browser-side analytics tracker that posted visitor events straight to PocketBase collections, plus `storage`/`content` module flags that `GET /api/features` advertised but nothing implemented. The tracker existed only to duplicate data PostgreSQL already owned — `PageView`/`LinkClick` are written server-side behind the same consent and Do-Not-Track/GPC gate, aggregated into the analytics dashboard and pruned after 90 days, whereas the PocketBase copies had no read path, no retention job and no way to erase a visitor's data on request, and the browser-written path was an unauthenticated public-INSERT surface governed by PocketBase's own rule engine. Use the optional Matomo integration for external analytics and the built-in B2/S3/local storage drivers for files. **If you ran a build that had the PocketBase analytics module enabled:** delete the `analytics_pageviews` and `analytics_linkclicks` collections in the PocketBase admin UI. They hold visitor identifiers, user agents, referrers and the full clicked URL, none of it pruned, and nothing has read them since analytics moved to PostgreSQL — so deleting them loses no functionality. Keep the `users` auth collection, which is where the sign-in provider keeps its credentials. **Tests** — `tests/pocketbase.test.ts` (config flags, modern + legacy superuser auth with token caching) and `tests/pocketbase-oauth.test.ts` (18 tests: config flags, short/PB-refused tokens, already-linked login, linked-identity 2FA, verified-email auto-link and unverified-account verify, invite/no-invite provisioning, unverified-email `needs_setup`, invalid-invite 400, signup completion, link/idempotent-link/cross-account-409/unlink). Full backend suite 449/449, typecheck + lint + build clean. Docs: en/es `environment-variables.md` rewritten around the sign-in-only role with a removed-variable table and the operator cleanup instruction; en/es `api.md` needed no change (it only ever documented the OAuth endpoints).
- **Deemed acceptance for updated Terms/Privacy (consent-current, Phase 5)** — when Terms/Privacy are revised, an existing user who never re-checks is **deemed to have accepted** the new version by **continued use**: the platform no longer forces a fresh one-time re-consent prompt — instead, `POLICY_VERSIONS` was bumped to `2026-09-30` (Terms + Privacy, matching the frontend "Last updated: September 30, 2026" and the OpenAPI `PolicyVersions`), and a user is current on consent (`consentCurrent`) when either (a) they explicitly pinned the current `tos/privacy` versions, or (b) their **last login** is on/after `deemedAcceptanceCutoff(policyEffectiveDate())` — effective date + `DEEMED_ACCEPTANCE_DAYS` (default **30 days**), i.e. logging in again ≥ 30 days after the bump proves continued use (evidence, Terms §14 + Privacy §8: "Changes… Deemed Acceptance"). **Deemed-acceptance evidence** — `User.lastLoginAt` (new `last_login_at` column, added non-destructively `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS` to **live + test** DBs — no force-reset/migrate) is stamped on every successful login (`authGuard`); the admin consent search and the per-user consent record now surface `lastLoginAt` so operators can see *why* a user is deemed current. Consent pins still record the exact `tosVersion`/`privacyVersion` accepted (≥ `POLICY_VERSIONS`), the original pinned **versions + timestamp are never overwritten** (admin consent evidence keeps original rows), and Terms/Privacy "Last updated" headers were bumped to match. OpenAPI + admin consent docs + en/es policies updated.
- **Registration consent + platform announcements broadcast** — the register flow now requires a single combined **"I have read and agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy"** acceptance checkbox (`acceptedPolicies: true` in `POST /auth/register`); the user record stores the accepted policy versions + timestamp (`acceptedTosVersion`/`acceptedPrivacyVersion`/`acceptedPoliciesAt`). An **optional** platform-announcements checkbox opts the account into platform news (`newsletterOptIn`). Users manage the preference with a **Dashboard → Email → Platform announcements** toggle (`POST /api/newsletter/optin`). **Admin broadcast** — **Admin → Newsletter → Platform announcements** lets instance admins send one announcement to every opted-in account through the instance SMTP/Resend sender: audience = `newsletterOptIn=true AND broadcastUnsubscribedAt=null`, safety cap `ADMIN_BROADCAST_RECIPIENT_CAP` (default 5000), subject ≤ 120 / body ≤ 5000 (sanitized), each send recorded in a new `admin_newsletter_sends` table; endpoints `GET /api/admin/newsletter/broadcast-audience`, `GET /api/admin/newsletter/broadcasts`, `POST /api/admin/newsletter/broadcast`. Every broadcast email carries a **signed, account-level one-click unsubscribe** link — `GET /api/newsletter/unsubscribe/broadcast?token=` (JWT purpose `broadcast_unsubscribe`) turns the opt-in off and stores `broadcastUnsubscribedAt` (CAN-SPAM/CASL compliant); re-opt-in is always possible from account settings. - **Product shop (Phase 5)** — per-profile digital-product storefront: the owner lists priceable files and visitors buy them with any enabled gateway and download instantly. **Owner side (Dashboard → Shop)** — a profile can carry up to 3 products on FREE (PRO/Enterprise unlimited). Products are one deliverable file (uploaded via `POST /api/shop/products`, capped by `PRODUCT_FILE_MAX_MB`, default 50) with a title (≤ 80, sanitized), description (≤ 500, sanitized) and a price (`priceCents`, 0 = free), plus an optional preview image (`POST /api/shop/products/:id/preview`, 5 MB, JPEG/PNG/GIF/WebP, stored as a public upload). A per-profile **discount percent** (`shopDiscountPercent`, 0–100 on profile update) prices every product after the server-side discount; the platform's price/discount model is **hybrid**: per-product prices on top of a profile-wide promo. Products render a **shop section** on the public profile (only enabled products, safe fields only). The public shop shows a grid of cards (preview or gradient, title, description, discounted price with the original crossed out) and a buy dialog: payment method (Card / PayPal / Crypto when the instance has them), coin picker for crypto, and an email field — required for paid purchases (guests get the download link by email), optional for free ones (free downloads respond immediately). **Payment** — `POST /api/shop/buy` prices server-side and routes to Stripe/PayPal/crypto checkout (`createStripeCheckout`/`createPayPalOrder` gained `successUrl`/`cancelUrl`, defaulting to the order flow) or marks a free product PAID with method `FREE`; gateway webhooks branch on the `purchase-<id>` order prefix in `handleGatewayEvent` and transition the purchase (paid → available, paid → refunded → revoked, cancel-on-PAID ignored). **Delivery** — signed JWTs (`purpose=product_download`, TTL `PRODUCT_DOWNLOAD_TTL_HOURS`, default 168 h; client `purpose=product_purchase` token TTL `PRODUCT_PURCHASE_TOKEN_TTL_DAYS`, default 30 d) gate `GET /api/shop/download/:id?token=` (auth Bearer works too): served as `application/octet-stream` + `filename*` attachment, `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, refunded → 410, confirmed-but-download-expired → 402. Deliverable files live under a **private** `products/` storage subtree (blocked from the public `/uploads` serve path; `media.ts isSafeUploadRel`), uploaded via `publishProductUpload` (cloud providers write provider objects `products/<uuid>`), cleanup via `deleteProductUpload` (profile delete returns them too). **Buyer side** — Dashboard gained a **Purchases** tab listing the logged-in buyer's purchases with re-download; **Sales** — the Shop tab lists every purchase for the profile (buyer email masked, e.g. `a***@domain`, product, method, price, discount, status) while the buyer's own status polling uses the returned client token or `checkout.url` to redirect and auto-complete, and a PAID poll returns the download URL (success/cancelled gateway returns land back on the profile with a `?shop=purchase&status=` notice). Rate limits: buy 30/h, status 120/h, download 60/h per IP. Backend: `Product` + `ProductPurchase` models (`PurchaseStatus` `PENDING|PAID|REFUNDED|CANCELLED`, `PurchaseMethod` `STRIPE|PAYPAL|CRYPTO|FREE`; `docs/migrations/2026-09-16_shop.sql`, applied to test + live DBs), `lib/shop.ts`, `routes/shop.ts` mounted at `/api/shop`, public profile now carries `products` + `shopDiscountPercent`, OpenAPI `Shop` tag + paths + `shopDiscountPercent` field on the profile schemas; frontend `components/shop/ShopBlock.tsx` (public) + `ShopTab.tsx` (owner) + `PurchasesTab.tsx` (buyer), wired into `Dashboard.tsx` and `PublicProfile.tsx`; `tests/shop.test.ts` (16 cases); 314/314 backend tests pass; typecheck + frontend build clean.
- **Tips (Phase 4)** — per-profile cryptocurrency tips (Bitcoin + Litecoin), owner-managed and visible on the public profile. **Settings** — a profile owner enables tips, sets a heading and one or both wallet addresses (Bitcoin `1…`/`3…`/`bc1…`, Litecoin `L…`/`M…`/`ltc1…`, validated with strict address-shape regexes) in the new **Dashboard → Tips** tab. While tips are disabled no tip block renders and the wallet addresses stay `null` on the **public** profile payload (own-profile responses always include them), so a disabled wallet never leaks. **Public tip block** — visitors click a Tip button and a dialog opens: coin tabs (when both coins are configured), amount presets (0.001–0.5) or a custom amount, optional name (≤ 60) and message (≤ 500), then either a BTCPay checkout (when the instance has BTCPay configured) or the wallet's `bitcoin:`/`litecoin:` URI with a live QR code (scan → pay) plus a copy-address button. **Ledger** — `POST /api/tips` (public, rate-limited 30/h per IP, sanitized server-side) records the tip, and the Tips tab **Overview** cards show totals per coin — `recorded` (every tip intent) and `confirmed` (payments actually settled) — plus the 50 most recent tips with per-row delete. **Payment confirmation** — when BTCPay is configured (`CRYPTO_ENABLED` + `BTCPAY_*`, the same stack that fulfils plan orders), tip invoices use `orderId = "tip-<id>"`: the existing crypto webhook marks the tip `CONFIRMED` on `InvoiceSettled` and `CANCELLED` on `InvoiceExpired`/`InvoiceInvalid` (already-confirmed tips ignore later events); if BTCPay is absent or invoice creation fails, the tip stays a recorded address-mode intent (`mode: "address"`) that the owner reconciles against their own wallet. ### Fixed
- **Invite pack pricing could make a bulk pack a worse deal than the singles** — `INVITE_PRICE_PACKS` only lets a buyer purchase the exact quantities listed, so `1:100,3:200,10:800` was self-defeating: ten credits cost 800 minor units as a "10" pack, but a 3-pack plus a 1-pack bought the same ten credits for 700, making the largest pack the worst value while the storefront marked the *3-pack* as best value. The live and example configurations are now `1:100,3:200,10:600` (60 minor units a credit against the 3-pack's 67), which makes the bulk pack the marked best value. `en`/`es` admin guides and environment docs now state the rule and note that this variable needs a **container restart** to change, unlike the database-backed switches.

- **Cookie preferences did nothing under Do Not Track** — the footer link was dead for every visitor whose browser sends DNT/GPC (the default in several browsers), in two compounding ways. `CookieConsentBanner` returned `null` whenever `dnt` was set, *before* checking whether the visitor had explicitly opened the panel, so clicking "cookie preferences" set state that rendered nothing; and `POST /api/privacy/consent` stored `essential` regardless of what was chosen (`decision = dnt ? "essential" : requested`), so even a visible button could never enable analytics. DNT is now a **default, not a veto**: it still applies to a visitor who has chosen nothing (`effective` reports `essential`), but an explicit "Accept all" or "Essential only" is recorded as given. The banner therefore opens on request under DNT, explains that the browser is blocking analytics and that accepting turns them on, and does not inherit the 20-second countdown that only exists for first-time visitors. `recordDecision` now returns the decision the **server** stored instead of only `res.ok`, so the UI can no longer claim analytics are allowed while the cookie says otherwise, and a failed save reopens the panel with an error instead of silently dropping the choice.
- **Storefront could contradict the legal page on resale** — the buy page derived its resale sentence from `mode !== "off"`, which prints a prohibition for any mode that is not `off`. With the new `permitted` value that would have been a false claim, so the storefront now renders from the server-sent `resale.clause`.

### Security
- **CI workflows no longer run with inherited write access to the whole repository** — neither `.github/workflows/tests.yml` nor the `changes` job of `.github/workflows/docker-publish.yml` declared a `permissions:` block, so both took whatever the repository default was (commonly read/write on *every* scope). That default is the token a workflow runs with, and it is handed to every step: a single compromised or malicious build-time dependency — the exact thing `pnpm install` and the Docker builds pull in — could have pushed branches, written releases and read every repository secret from a job whose only real job is to run tests and diff git history. Both workflows now declare `permissions: {}` at the top (deny everything) and re-grant the minimum per job: `contents: read` for the test gate and the change detector, plus `packages: write` kept only on `build-push`, which is the single job that pushes images. Every `actions/checkout` also sets `persist-credentials: false`, because neither workflow pushes with git (images go out through buildx with the registry logins), so leaving the job token in `.git/config` only exposed it to later steps. Found by CodeQL flagging both workflows for missing/unrestricted permissions.
- **Storefront rate limiting no longer lets read traffic starve checkout** — see Fixed (`/invite-purchases/config` shared the purchase limiter, so refreshing the store page could deny a paying buyer their own purchase). Reachable by any unauthenticated visitor with no secrets, so the exposure was availability rather than data; the fix keeps the tight budget on purchase creation and gives read-only routes their own.
- **The operator auto-accept rule is disclosed, bounded and recorded** — see Added. It is a grant of blanket legal acceptance, so it is deliberately narrow: default role list is the single built-in `admin`, it cannot fire before the same 30-day window everyone else gets, the outcome is written to the DB with a marker distinguishing it from a human click, the rule in force is visible in **Admin → Legal**, and `POLICY_ADMIN_AUTO_ACCEPT=false` disables it entirely. The 63 missing variables were not a secret leak, but they silently substituted schema defaults for prices and switches the operator had configured, which is a correctness and disclosure problem: the Terms of Service are dynamically generated from what the instance actually does, so a mis-forwarded variable can change what the legal pages claim.

- **Presence polling halved (15s → 30s).** Presence state stays centralized server-side (Discord gateway cache with a TTL sweep), so the client-side poll exists only to pick up live updates.
- **Dashboard render cost reduced.** The 30-day analytics chart series are built once with `useMemo` instead of inside the render body, and route pages are code-split via `React.lazy` + `Suspense` (the landing shell loads first; Dashboard, Admin, public profile, legal and API-docs chunks load on demand).
- **Analytics queries run in parallel.** The seven 30-day `$queryRaw` aggregates on `/api/analytics/me` now run via `Promise.all` instead of serially.
- **OG render avoids a double DB load.** `profileOgImageUrl` accepts an already-loaded profile data object, so `renderProfileOgPage` no longer re-reads the profile to build the card URL.
- **nginx upstream keepalive.** Upstream pools use `keepalive 32` and a `$connection_upgrade` map now drops the `Connection` header on non-WebSocket requests (previously every proxied request was sent with `Connection: upgrade`), letting nginx reuse upstream connections.
- **Scroll-reveal no longer animates `filter: blur`** (only opacity + transform), removing a per-frame paint cost on landing-page reveals.

### Fixed

- **Webhook retry sweep is now re-entrant and non-N+1.** The sweep guards against overlapping runs, fetches the webhook row in the same query as the due deliveries, processes them in `nextRetryAt` order, and starts only after the database connection is ready (previously it fired at module load).
- **Auth-log pruning is guarded against overlapping runs** (the interval could previously overlap and run two concurrent delete sweeps).
- **YouTube music player leaks no timers.** The start-with-sound timeout chain is now tracked and cleared when the player unmounts or the `started` flag flips, so a destroyed player can no longer receive a late mute/unmute tick.

### Security

- **TLS private keys and user data no longer enter Docker build contexts.** `.dockerignore` now excludes `certs/` (certificates and private keys), `uploads/` (avatars/banners), database dumps and backups, and local DB files — previously these were part of every build context sent to the Docker daemon.
- **ACME challenge tokens are bounded in memory.** The in-memory challenge map now stores a creation timestamp and prunes entries older than 10 minutes (also on each read), so a burst of challenge attempts can no longer grow it without limit.
- **Token-purpose confusion could bypass 2FA (CRITICAL).** `requireAuth` and `getViewerId` accepted *any* JWT signed with `JWT_SECRET` as a full session bearer — including the short-lived `purpose: "twofactor"` token issued after a password login and the `purpose: "unlock"` email-unlock token — so a password-only attacker could skip TOTP/passkey. Session tokens are now signed with `purpose: "auth"`, and both middlewares reject any other purpose (401).
- **Webhook SSRF via redirects and DNS rebinding (HIGH).** Delivery used `fetch()` with automatic redirect following, so `Location` targets (including HTTP downgrades) were never re-validated; and the hostname was resolved separately from the later connect, so DNS-rebinding and IPv4-mapped-IPv6 (`::ffff:7f00:1`) answers evaded the private-IP check. Delivery now follows redirects manually, re-validates every hop with `isSafeWebhookTarget`, enforces `MAX_REDIRECTS`, allows `https:` only, resolves once and pins the socket to the validated IP via `lookup`, and rejects every private/reserved range (RFC1918, CGNAT, link-local, loopback, multicast, NAT64, IPv4-mapped-IPv6, …) with a `net.BlockList`.
- **`X-Forwarded-For` was fully spoofable (HIGH).** `TRUST_PROXY=1` plus nginx echoing the client-supplied `X-Forwarded-For` let any visitor set their own `req.ip`, bypassing the public view/click rate limit, the auth fingerprint IP, and analytics. Nginx now overwrites `X-Forwarded-For`/`X-Real-IP` with the client IP it computed from trusted reverse-proxy sources, so a forged chain never reaches the backend. See *Changed* for the follow-up that makes the restoration generic and network-level unforgeable.
- **`POST /api/profiles/click` abuse (MEDIUM).** The click endpoint had no `isPublic` gate, no platform-existence check, and accepted any `profileId`, enabling DB-row spam, notify-on-click email floods, `link.clicked` webhook spam, and enumeration of private profiles by leaked UUID. Clicks now 404 unless the profile is public (owner still allowed), 400 if the platform is not in the profile's `socialLinks`, and a per-profile sliding-window rate limit (60/min) is applied before recording.
- **Privilege escalation via `users.manage` (MEDIUM).** Any `users.manage` holder could assign the system **admin** role or `ENTERPRISE` tier, and `reset-password` lacked a self-guard so the root admin's password could be reset. Role/tier changes now require `roles.manage`, the target role is rejected if it grants permissions beyond the caller's own (subset check), tier assignment cannot exceed the caller's own tier, and `reset-password` rejects `id === req.userId`.
- **`/unlock` enumeration + unthrottled email bomb (MEDIUM).** The unlock endpoint returned `sent:false` for unknown/not-locked accounts and `sent:true` for locked ones, leaking account existence with no rate limit. All cases now return the same `{ sent: true }`, a per-account email cooldown (10 min) silently skips duplicate sends, and a per-IP limit (5/hr) returns 429.
- **Brute-force bypass (MEDIUM).** `fingerprintBlock` only blocked when ≥2 fingerprint vectors were locked, and the `trusted_ip` policy skipped the account lock when the request IP matched `registeredIp`/`lastLoginIp`. Fingerprint blocking now triggers on any single locked vector, and account locks always apply regardless of request-IP equality.
- **Spreadsheet formula injection in export (MEDIUM).** `bio`/`displayName`/`location` were written raw into exported sheets, so leading `=`, `+`, `-`, `@` or tab/CR/LF executed in Excel/LibreOffice (the import path was guarded, export was not). All user-controlled export values now pass through `sanitizeExportValue`, which prefixes `'` for values matching the formula-injection signature; the import guard regex was extended with `-`.
- **ACME tick stall (MEDIUM).** `acmeTick` processed NULL-expiry (`FAILED`/`PENDING`) domains first, so one unreachable domain could monopolize a run and delay real renewals. The tick now splits the run — `ISSUED` domains actually expiring (`tlsExpiresAt <= renewBy`) first, then non-`ISSUED` domains to fill remaining capacity, with `PENDING`/`FAILED` retried only after a 60-minute backoff.
- **Client IP can no longer be faked from the network.** The nginx published ports (`NGINX_PORT`/`NGINX_HTTPS_PORT`) are now bound to loopback (`127.0.0.1`) in `docker-compose.yml` (matching postgres and the backend), so only host-local processes (cloudflared, localhost dev) can reach nginx — a remote attacker cannot connect directly to inject a forged `X-Forwarded-For`/`CF-Connecting-IP`; every request must arrive via the trusted reverse proxy. Local traffic through docker-proxy (which masquerades its source as the docker bridge gateway) is surfaced as `127.0.0.1` instead of the gateway address. Residual trust boundary: a process already on the host or a sibling container on the same bridge could still forge the header, and binding the ports to `0.0.0.0` voids this guarantee.

- CSP `img-src` now also allows `https://cdn.discordapp.com` (Discord avatars + activity app icons) and `https://i.scdn.co` (Spotify album art) so the presence widget renders — the strict allowlist otherwise stays unchanged
- HTTPS served on 443 with HSTS (production mode only; `SEND_HSTS_ON_DEV=true` opts in for dev). `TLS_MODE=development` auto-generates self-signed certs stored as `self-signed.pem`/`self-signed.key`; `TLS_MODE=production` deletes them and requires real certs- `JWT_SECRET` must be at least 32 characters (backend refuses to boot with a weak secret)
- Profile `theme` values are validated server-side: colors must be hex / `rgb()` / `rgba()` / `hsl()` / `hsla()` and `fontFamily` is restricted to a safe character set — rejects CSS injection (`url()`, `var()`, `calc()`, `;`, `{}`) before it reaches inline styles
- Login responses made uniform to stop user enumeration: `/auth/login/start` always reports a found account with password method, and `/auth/login/passkey/options` + 2FA endpoints return a generic 401 "Invalid credentials" instead of 404/distinct messages for unknown users
- Email unlock (`/auth/unlock/verify`) now clears the account's IP/cookie fingerprint bans and failed auth-log entries alongside the account ban, matching the admin unlock behavior
- Webhook signing secrets are never returned after creation (shown once) and are stored AES-256-GCM encrypted; delivery signatures are HMAC-SHA256 over the raw body
- Spreadsheet imports reject macro-bearing files (`.xlsm`/`.xls`) and values that could be interpreted as formulas (leading `=`, `+`, `@`, tab/CR); webhook payloads and profile exports contain no PII
- Discord OAuth access/refresh tokens and webhook URLs are stored AES-256-GCM encrypted with purpose-scoped keys (`bioplatform:discord:token` / `...:webhook`); presence is never exposed unless the owner explicitly opts in (`showDiscordPresence`, `showDiscordActivity`), OAuth state tokens are signed with `JWT_SECRET` and expire after 10 minutes, and the shared presence bot token is read only from the environment (never stored or logged), with fatal gateway close codes (invalid token / disallowed intents) disabling the bot instead of retrying blindly
- Profile slugs and aliases are validated against the same allowlist pattern (`^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-_]{2,31}$`), case-normalized to lowercase, and sanitized before storage; primary slugs cannot be renamed and reserved collisions are rejected
- Every admin endpoint now checks a specific permission (`requirePermission`) tied to the caller's role instead of a single admin flag, so restricted roles can't reach operations they don't own
> **Accepted / deferred risks:** the JWT stays in `localStorage` (XSS-exposed) with the strict `script-src 'self'` CSP as the compensating control — an HttpOnly-cookie refactor is deferred. `/auth/login/start` still reveals a real account's enabled methods (passkey/totp), intentional for the username-first UX. Production operators must deploy real TLS certs + HSTS and rotate the pre-existing admin password created before the seed fix. Webhook delivery runs in-process (no separate worker queue); long endpoint delays hold the retry sweep and the 5-min sweep interval is the lower bound between attempts.
